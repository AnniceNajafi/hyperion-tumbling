What the project found

Period search6.6 d, FAP ≈ 0.07

The strongest period in Klavetter's data is not significant, and a periodic model leaves 0.11 mag of scatter, more than ten times the error bars.

Planar model28 d

Lyapunov time in the planar chaotic sea, which spans spin rates from near zero to 2.5 times the orbital rate.

Attitude instability~15 orbits

A 10⁻⁴ rad tilt of synchronous rotation grows past 10°. Planar rotation cannot last, as Wisdom et al. predicted in 1984.

Observed 3D stateweakly chaotic

Near the Voyager/Cassini spin of 4.2 times the orbital rate, 62–75% of runs are chaotic by two shear-robust tests (window exponent and MEGNO), with median Lyapunov times of 130–430 d. A strongly chaotic subset (12–46%) has 78–152 d, like the published values. The state persists for decades in our runs.

Open questionslow or fast?

The regular models we tested are disfavoured, simple periodic rotation only at the few-per-cent level. The 1987 data favour slow tumbling, while spacecraft saw fast spin in 1981 and 2005.

Models: planar spin–orbit equation (Wisdom, Peale & Mignard 1984) and full rigid-body Euler equations with gravity-gradient torque on a fixed Kepler orbit, RK4 with 512 steps per orbit. Moments from Thomas et al. (1995) and Harbison et al. (2011); data from Klavetter (1989), AJ 97, 570.

3D shape: Cassini plate model by P. Thomas, J. Joseph and T. Ansty, Saturn Small Moon Shape Models V1.0, NASA Planetary Data System (2018), rotated into its principal axes. Its uniform-density moments, 0.313 / 0.476 / 0.542, match the Cassini values above. The shape is real to within about 1 km where imaging was best; the surface colouring is shading by local concavity, not a measured albedo map. Brightness is the sunlit projected area at small phase angle, with the Sun taken along the Earth direction and shadowing inside craters ignored.

Distances in the orbit view are compressed and Hyperion is drawn enlarged.
